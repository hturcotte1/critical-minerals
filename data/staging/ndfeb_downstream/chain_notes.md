# NdFeB downstream notes (workstream ndfeb_downstream), as of 2026-11-16

Scope: metal/alloy, sintered magnets, bonded/hot-deformed magnets and MQ-type powders, recycling, ancillary inputs (boron/ferroboron, high-purity iron) and rare-earth-free substitutes. Mining and separation are in `ndfeb_upstream`; SmCo is in `smco_cobalt`.

Research constraint: the shared web-search budget for this session ran out early. After that, facts were checked only by fetching company, filing and government pages directly (no search-engine queries). Rows that rest on older or thinner sources have `confidence` low/medium and say so in `notes`.

File totals: 65 supplier rows (24 sintered, 6 bonded/hot-deformed/powder, 12 metal/alloy, 18 recycling, 3 ancillary inputs, 2 substitutes), 39 offtakes, 16 excluded covered-country facilities, 103 sources.

## Who matters at each stage (non-covered-country only)

**Metal / alloy**
- Energy Fuels / ASM Korean Metals Plant (Ochang, Korea): 1,300 t/yr NdFeB alloy now, 3,600 t/yr targeted from end-2026. It is the main merchant non-China alloy seller (binding POs to Noveon, VAC, Magnequench in 2025). It will likely become captive to VAC once Energy Fuels closes the VAC deal (early 2027).
- Less Common Metals (Ellesmere Port, UK; USA Rare Earth since Nov 2025): long-running ex-China metal and strip-cast maker, increasingly captive to USAR's Stillwater plant. LCM Europe plans a 3,750 t/yr plant at Lacq (late 2028).
- MP Materials Independence (Fort Worth): NdPr metal and alloy from its own Mountain Pass oxide. It is the only fully US mine-to-metal chain, but the output is captive.
- Saskatchewan Research Council (Saskatoon): NdPr metal upgrade to about 525 t/yr from 2027; about 80% is contracted to REalloys. REalloys' heavy rare earth metallization facility targets about 30 t/yr Dy and 15 t/yr Tb metal in 2028.
- Phoenix Tailings (Exeter, NH): an operating commercial metallization plant (capacity not published). It plans the Freedom Facility in Rochester, NY for 2028 (OSC conditional $500M).
- Planned: USAR Blacksburg, SC (5,000-6,400 t/yr, 2028); Neo Project GAUSS metallization in Estonia (about 2029); Energy Fuels American Metals Plant (OSC-backed, no site yet).

**Sintered magnets**
- MP Materials: Independence (about 1,000 t/yr, GBD, GM deliveries from Q4 2026) and 10X in Northlake (2028, fully covered by the DoD offtake guarantee).
- e-VAC Magnetics (Sumter, SC): 2,000 t/yr since fall 2025. GM terminated its roughly 90% offtake in Oct 2026 and the dispute is in arbitration. e-VAC is DLA-qualified.
- USA Rare Earth Stillwater (OK): 600 t/yr since 2026, rising to 1,200 t/yr in Q2 2027. First sales were in Sep 2026. Funded by Commerce CHIPS ($277M grant plus up to $1.3B loans).
- Noveon Magnetics (San Marcos, TX): a DLA-qualified sintered producer with recycled feed. Customers include Nidec, GM and ABB.
- Vulcan Elements: Research Triangle Park, NC (operating) and Benson, NC (10,000 t/yr, commissioning 2027; OSC $620M conditional).
- Neo Performance Materials, Narva (Estonia): 2,000 t/yr, commercial since Sep 2026, with an expansion to about 5,000 t/yr planned. VAC also makes magnets in Hanau (Germany), Horna Streda (Slovakia) and Ulvila (Finland, Neorem).
- Japan and Vietnam: Shin-Etsu (Takefu; Haiphong, Vietnam), Proterial (NEOMAX), TDK. These are large incumbents with undisclosed raw material sources; Shin-Etsu runs a 3,000 t/yr alloy plant in Changting, China. Proterial plans a 1,200 t/yr plant in Andhra Pradesh, India (Jul 2026).

**Bonded / hot-deformed / MQ powder**
- Neo Magnequench, Korat (Thailand): 2,000 t/yr bonded powder. It is the only sizeable non-China MQ powder plant; Neo's Tianjin, China plant has 6,000 t/yr.
- Daido Electronics, Nakatsugawa (Japan) and Ayutthaya (Thailand): hot-deformed magnets that can be made without heavy rare earths (HEV traction motors).
- Aichi Steel MAGFINE (Japan): anisotropic bonded magnets free of Dy and Tb. Also MS-Schramberg (Germany) and Magneti Ljubljana (Slovenia). Powder origin is not disclosed by any of these.

**Recycling**
- Noveon M2M (US milling and sintering, which fits the DFARS recycled-magnet exemption).
- HyProMag: Tyseley, UK (operating 2026) and Pforzheim, Germany (opened Apr 2026, EU strategic project). HyProMag USA in Northlake, TX will finish magnets from H1 2027 and start integrated HPMS recycling and sintering in mid-2028 (EXIM LOI $92M).
- Cyclic Materials: Mesa, AZ (opened Sep 2026, 25,000 t/yr of magnet-bearing products) and Kingston, ON (500 t/yr). It has a 10-year swarf deal with VAC.
- ReElement (Marion and Noblesville, IN): toll-refines recycled magnets to oxides. DoW awarded it $25M (Jul 2026).
- Carester Caremag (Lacq, France): 1,600 t/yr, commissioning targeted for late 2026. Customers include Stellantis and Neo (heavy rare earths).
- Smaller players: USA Rare Earth Wheat Ridge (demonstration), Momentum Technologies (Carrollton, TX demonstration), Ionic Technologies (Belfast, planned commercial plant), Geomega (Quebec demonstration), MagREEsource (France).

**Ancillary inputs**
- Boron is plentiful outside China: Rio Tinto/U.S. Borax at Boron, CA (about 1 Mt/yr refined borates, about 30% of world demand) and Eti Maden in Turkey (Turkey produced 1,500 kt in 2025; USGS MCS 2026).
- Ferroboron conversion is the gap. DOE (2022) says the US makes no ferroboron (2017 data) and imports about 665 t/yr, and that ferroboron is produced in China, India and Turkey. No named non-China ferroboron plant could be verified in this session.
- High-purity iron: Hertha Metals (Texas) plans a 10,000 t/yr plant for steel-grade and magnet-grade iron. It raised $134M, including $65M from the US government.

**Substitutes**
- Niron Magnetics (iron nitride): a commercial pilot in Minneapolis and a 1,500 t/yr plant in Sartell, MN under construction (steel raised Sep 2026). DoW made a conditional $150M commitment (Aug 2026) and Honda has invested.

## The real bottleneck

The binding constraint for 2027 is non-China **heavy rare earths (Dy/Tb) and the metal/alloy step**, not sintering capacity.
- USA Rare Earth reported that Q3 2026 output at LCM and Stillwater was limited by shortages of Dy oxide and Sm metal. It put non-China Dy oxide at 14-16x the China price (USAR Q3 2026, SEC 8-K Ex. 99.1).
- Western Dy oxide averaged $365/kg in 2025 and just over $400/kg by mid-2026. Tripling non-China NdFeB would need about 3x the NdPr oxide and about 10x the Dy/Tb oxide within about 4 years. Metal/alloy capacity is named as a near-term bottleneck (Adamas conference recap, Magnetics Magazine, 3 Nov 2026).
- Merchant non-China alloy is roughly KMP's 1,300 t/yr (3,600 t/yr from end-2026) plus LCM (capacity not published). Non-China sintered capacity is about 20,000+ t/yr (see below). Most new magnet plants therefore rely on a handful of metal makers or on undisclosed sources.
- Verified heavy rare earth metal capacity outside China is near zero before 2028. REalloys' facility (45 t/yr Dy+Tb, 2028) and KMP's Dy/Tb metallization (in development) are the only identified projects.
- GBD is widespread among serious producers (MP, VAC, Shin-Etsu), and MP reports about 60% less heavy rare earth than first planned. It reduces Dy/Tb demand but does not remove it.

## Uncommitted non-China capacity for 2027

**Sintered capacity.** Basis: the Adamas/Neo market figures and company nameplates, not production.
- Global NdFeB demand was about 259,000 t in 2025, and China made about 93% of sintered magnets (Neo Q2 2026 presentation, citing Adamas Dec 2025). That puts non-China sintered output in 2025 at roughly 16,000-17,000 t, mostly in Japan and Vietnam.
- New US and EU nameplate for 2027 adds about 6,000 t: MP 1,000, e-VAC 2,000, USAR 1,200, Neo Narva 2,000. Vulcan Benson's 10,000 t is due to commission in 2027, but its 2027 output is uncertain.
- Best estimate of non-China sintered nameplate in 2027: about **22,000-25,000 t/yr** (Japan, Vietnam and Europe incumbents about 17,000 t, plus new plants). This is an order-of-magnitude figure; Japanese capacities are not published.

**Share on non-China oxide and metal.** Only about **1,000-3,000 t/yr** can be shown to run on a documented non-China mine-to-metal chain in 2027.
- MP Independence (about 1,000 t) is fully non-China but committed.
- Narva uses partly Silmet and Carester oxides, but its metal source is undisclosed.
- e-VAC's feed is undisclosed, and VAC's German plant buys Chinese flake.
- The Japanese and Vietnamese incumbents (Shin-Etsu, TDK, Proterial) disclose no sources, and Shin-Etsu runs a 3,000 t/yr alloy plant in China.

**Uncommitted share.** About **3,000-4,000 t/yr** of identifiable uncommitted sintered capacity in 2027:
- e-VAC Sumter: about 1,800 t freed by GM's termination; DLA order only.
- USAR Stillwater: 600-1,200 t, no named offtakes.
- Parts of Noveon, Narva and Vulcan RTP.
- Vulcan Benson ramp volumes: unknown, potentially large.

Almost none of this uncommitted capacity has a fully documented non-China oxide and metal chain. Closing that documentation gap is the broker's job.

**Defense demand for scale.** DLA's FY2025 Annual Materials Plan listed potential acquisitions of 450 t of NdFeB magnet block and 300 t of NdPr oxide (USGS MCS 2026). DOE (2022) notes military magnet needs are well below the capacity of a single moderate-scale plant.

## Gaps: stages with zero or near-zero compliant supply

- **Ferroboron:** no verified non-China producer in the file (DOE: China, India, Turkey). The US has boron (Rio Tinto) but no ferroboron conversion.
- **Heavy rare earth metals (Dy, Tb, DyFe, TbFe):** no verified operating non-China merchant capacity before 2028.
- **Bonded and MQ powder:** Magnequench Korat (2,000 t) is the only identified non-China powder plant; Tianjin, China (6,000 t) dominates. No bonded or hot-deformed producer documents a non-China chain.
- **Japanese incumbents:** no public evidence of China-free sourcing; all are at_risk.
- **High-purity iron:** the US imports nearly all; Hertha is only at construction stage.
